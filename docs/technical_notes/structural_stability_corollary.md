# Local structural stability of the certified exchange

Let the two strong amplitudes be \(1-\eta\) and \(1+\eta\), let the weak location be \(b\), and let its phase be \(\phi\). Fix one of the certified record lengths. At the symmetric point \((b,\phi,\eta)=(10,\pi,0)\), the A/B roots are separated, their Gram matrices and reduced Hessians are positive, their satellite coefficients are nonzero, their equal-cost slope is nonzero, and every other fitted subspace has a strict global cost margin.

**Corollary.** For each such fixed certified instance there is an (unspecified) open neighborhood of \((10,\pi,0)\) in which two separated regular branches retain a unique transverse global exchange after adjusting the crossing amplitude. This is an existence statement, not a validated radius or a global parameter atlas.

**Finite \(z=2\) proof.** In a neighborhood of each noncoalescent root, the projected residual and its derivatives depend continuously on \((b,\phi,\eta,\epsilon)\). Positive Gram and Hessian determinants permit the implicit-function theorem to continue each root. The nonzero A/B cost-difference derivative permits a unique nearby equal-cost amplitude. Outside disjoint root neighborhoods, the frequency torus together with its confluent closure is compact and the certified strict exclusion margin has a positive minimum. Uniform continuity preserves half of that margin after a sufficiently small parameter perturbation. The two continued roots are therefore the only globally competitive fits. This applies separately at the \(N=21\) and \(N=31\) certified \(z=2\) points.

**Small-spacing proof.** Asymmetry requires recentering before taking the tangent limit. Indeed,
\[
(1-\eta)e^{-izs}+(1+\eta)e^{izs}
=2e^{i\eta zs}-(1-\eta^2)z^2s^2+O(z^3).
\]
Thus, after removing the central tone \(2e^{i\eta zs}\) and setting \(\epsilon=\lambda z^2\), the leading tangent datum is
\[
q(s)=-(1-\eta^2)s^2+\lambda e^{i\phi}e^{ibs}.
\]
The tangent Gram blocks, two regular roots, crossing equation, and strict competitor gaps vary continuously with the three perturbation parameters. C1–C7 and the exhaustive compactification in C8 therefore persist locally for each fixed certified \(N\). Desingularization and the implicit-function theorem yield a positive small-\(z\) neighborhood. The critical law at fixed \(\eta\ne0\) need not retain the symmetric even-power \(z^4\) correction: odd powers may appear because the recentered record has an \(O(z^3)\) term.

The neighborhood sizes are not computed. This argument does not extend the \(z=2\) full-domain certificate to a displayed rectangle in \((b,\phi,\eta)\), does not claim a uniform neighborhood over all large \(N\), and does not certify the numerical phase map.
